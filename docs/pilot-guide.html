1. The big picture

PI 360 helps placement officers move a young person from training to work, and keeps an honest record of what happened. Four ideas run through it:

  • Opportunities are the jobs, training places or attachments employers offer.
  • Referrals put one youth forward for one opportunity and track what happens next.
  • Rules are the written, versioned criteria the system uses to suggest and explain. People approve them.
  • Cases (support interventions) are how officers act on a youth who needs extra help.
One sentence to remember: the system suggests and explains; a person always decides, and every change leaves a trail of who did what and when.

2. Opportunities Built, ready to try

An opportunity belongs to an employer and describes the role: title, kind, closing date and its requirements. Each requirement is typed (skill, qualification, location, availability, other) and marked mandatory or preferred.

Why it works this way

  • A closing date is the last open day. The day after, a daily job marks it expired, and referrals are blocked even before the job runs.
  • Closed and expired opportunities are locked so the record of what was offered cannot be rewritten after referrals were made.
  • Mandatory versus preferred is what the compatibility rules read later: a mandatory requirement can make a youth not eligible; a preferred one only adds to a score.
Pilot caveat: requirements are currently written as free text. Matching (Module B) will need structured values, for example a number of months or a skill with a level. That is part of the Month 3 work.

3. Referrals Built, ready to try

A referral is a staff member putting one youth forward for one opportunity. From then on it moves through stages, and every move is recorded.

The rules of the road

  • One live referral per youth and opportunity. No accidental duplicates.
  • Unsuccessful ends need a reason code from a controlled list, so the pilot can later learn why placements fail rather than reading free text.
  • History is append-only. A mistake is fixed by a correction that needs a written note and points at the entry it fixes. The original stays visible.
  • Reopening a closed referral needs a manager. Officers cannot undo a closure on their own.
  • Scope is respected. An officer only sees referrals for youth in their own caseload; others look like they do not exist.

4. Rules Built, ready to try

A rule set is one thing the system works out. It has numbered versions. A version is a list of factors (the checks) and parameters (the numbers those checks use, such as "how many days is too long").

Three rule sets, one per module

ModuleQuestion it answersWhat it produces
A. Support priorityWho may need extra follow-up?A flag with the reason, placed in a staff review queue. Never limits access to opportunities.
B. CompatibilityHow well does this youth fit this opportunity?Mandatory checks: eligible, eligible with unknowns, or not eligible. Preferred factors: a score plus how much data it was based on. The officer accepts or overrides, with a reason.
C. Employer reliabilityHow has this employer responded in the past?Indicators (response rate, interview and placement conversion, early retention), each with its count, period and source. Hidden as "insufficient evidence" when there are too few records.

The honest part: the drafts have structure, not numbers

The three drafts are loaded and visible under Rules. They say which factors exist and what data each reads. Every threshold and weight is an undecided parameter, labelled with the decision that will set it. The system refuses to approve a version while any parameter is undecided, so nothing can go live on a guess.

5. Cases and support interventions Planned, Month 3

A case here means a support intervention for a youth who needs help: for example a missing document, a skills gap, or a long time without a transition. It is how an officer turns a flag into action and keeps track of it.

  • Each intervention has an owner, a due date, the source of the evidence, a follow-up and a status.
  • Work queues give each officer their list, with overdue items highlighted.
  • Flags are prompts. Staff can acknowledge, dismiss or convert them. A flag never changes what a youth can be referred to.
Not built yet. Today an officer can manage the youth profile and referrals. Interventions and queues arrive in Month 3 (T-064), then Module A (T-063) feeds them. Placements, employment episodes and follow-up scheduling (T-065, T-066) follow.
